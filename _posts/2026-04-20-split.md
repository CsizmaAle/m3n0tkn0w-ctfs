## **Recognition Pattern**
- "Follow the flow" in the description was the hint — the flag's spread across a conversation, so I need to reconstruct the stream in order
- I found one piece of the flag in a single packet, then realised it was part of a back-and-forth between two devices → follow the whole thing, don't hunt packet by packet

## **Approach and Methodology**
### **Initial Analysis**
First I checked the file with `binwalk` to make sure it's just a `.pcap` with nothing hidden, then opened it in Wireshark to search for the flag.  

### **Steps Taken**
Opened the file in Wireshark (`wireshark challenge.pcap`), then `Ctrl+F`. Set the filter to **Packet bytes** and **String**, and entered `UVT` (the first part of the flag format). Hitting enter found the first part of the flag.  

Once I found the first part and realized the flag was part of a conversation between two devices, I decided to follow the conversation from the beginning.  

### **Alternative Approaches (if applicable)**
When searching for the flag part, the transmitted data in the packet is shown in hex. One way to solve this is to copy the data and convert it to text with a converter — but Wireshark already does that for you.  

## **Solution and Flag Retrieval**
*Final Exploit / Command Used:* used the down arrow until I found the second part of the flag, then put the two parts together.  

## **Lessons Learned**
- The main lesson: when I find one part of a flag in a packet, follow the whole conversation from the start instead of arrowing through packets one at a time.
- Searching in Wireshark, setting the find filter to **Packet bytes** + **String** and typing the flag prefix (`UVT`) is what got me the first hit. Worth remembering as the go-to way in.
- The transmitted data shows up as hex, but Wireshark already gives you the readable text alongside it — I didn't need any separate converter like I first assumed.
